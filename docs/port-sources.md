# Historical source note for each port

One verifiable source per port, with a single sentence on why the place matters around 1500. Notes are written in our own words; the links point to the supporting material. Checked 23 October 2026.

| Port | Source | Why it matters around 1500 |
|---|---|---|
| Cádiz | [Cádiz (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C%C3%A1diz) | A deep, sheltered Atlantic bay that was Castile's rising ocean port, soon to share Seville's America trade. |
| Sevilla | [Seville (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seville) | River port on the Guadalquivir; the Casa de Contratación (1503) made it the monopoly hub of the American trade. |
| Lisboa | [Lisbon (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lisbon) | Europe's gateway to the India spice route after da Gama's return in 1499, with island sugar flowing in as well. |
| Valencia | [Valencia (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valencia) | The commercial capital of the Crown of Aragon in the western Mediterranean; its silk trade built the Lonja de la Seda. |
| Barcelona | [Barcelona (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barcelona) | Centre of Catalan cloth making and Mediterranean shipping under the Consolat de Mar, though slowly losing ground around 1500. |
| Palma | [Palma de Mallorca (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palma_de_Mallorca) | Mallorca's merchants linked Iberia, Italy and North Africa with their own maritime consulate, trading salt, oil and saffron. |
| Bilbao | [Bilbao (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bilbao) | Outlet for Castilian wool and Basque iron to Flanders; the consulado of 1511 formalised a trade already running. |
| Tánger | [Tangier (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tangier) | Portuguese since 1471, it watches the Atlantic side of the Strait and ties Morocco's trade to Iberia. |
| Cartagena | [Cartagena, Spain (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cartagena,_Spain) | One of the best natural harbours in the western Mediterranean; around 1500 a modest exporter of esparto, salt and fish. |
| Oporto | [Porto (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porto) | Shipped Douro wines and northern cloth; already Portugal's second trading city around 1500. |
| Setúbal | [Setúbal (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Set%C3%BAbal) | Sado-estuary port whose sailors crewed the first ocean runs: local navigator Diogo Fernandes Pereira reached Socotra in 1503 and the Mascarenes in 1507. |
| Faro | [Faro, Portugal (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faro,_Portugal) | The Algarve's main outlet for tuna fisheries and salt, a small but steady customs port. |
| Huelva | [Huelva (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huelva) | Neighbour of Palos and La Rábida; Columbus's 1492 fleet sailed from its district, tying it to the Atlantic crossings. |
| Málaga | [Málaga (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C3%A1laga) | Taken by Castile in 1487, it had been the Nasrid kingdom's main outlet for wine, silk and dried fruit. |
| Almería | [Almería (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Almer%C3%ADa) | Once the Nasrid silk port par excellence; under Castile after 1489 it still exported silk, salt and esparto. |
| Alicante | [Alicante (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alicante) | Levantine harbour exporting wine and salt, a regular stop for coasters on the Valencian run. |
| Ibiza | [Ibiza (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibiza) | Its salt pans, worked since antiquity, made the island a strategic salt supplier of the western Mediterranean. |
| Tarragona | [Tarragona (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tarragona) | The old Roman capital exported wine and olive oil from a fertile plain, though its port was modest around 1500. |
| Santander | [Santander, Spain (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santander,_Spain) | Cantabrian harbour shipping wool and iron toward Flanders and France on Castile's northern route. |
| San Sebastián | [San Sebastián (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Sebasti%C3%A1n) | Basque port for iron and whale products on the wool route to Flanders, repeatedly rebuilt after fires. |
| La Coruña | [A Coruña (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Coru%C3%B1a) | A sheltered Galician harbour on the Atlantic route north; around 1500 a fishing and coasting port of growing value. |
| Vigo | [Vigo (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vigo) | Deep ría harbour living off fish; its wartime fame came later, but its fisheries were long established. |
| Ceuta | [Ceuta (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ceuta) | Held by Portugal since 1415, the first conquest of the overseas expansion, controlling the Strait opposite Gibraltar. |
| Melilla | [Melilla (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melilla) | Castilian since 1497, a fortress-port watching the North African coast at the edge of the Rif. |
| Tetuán | [Tétouan (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T%C3%A9touan) | Refounded in the late 15th century by refugees from Granada; its corsair fame grew later. |
| Arcila | [Asilah (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asilah) | Portuguese since 1471, an Atlantic Moroccan port trading into the Strait zone. |
| Larache | [Larache (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Larache) | Atlantic port at the Loukkos mouth exporting grain and timber, contested by Iberian powers over the next century. |
| Rabat | [Rabat (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rabat) | With its sister Salé on the Bou Regreg it exported wool and grain; its corsair era belongs to the 1600s. |
| Casablanca | [Casablanca (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casablanca) | Anfa's roadstead had long shipped the grain and wool of its plain, but the Portuguese razed the town in 1468 and around 1500 it still lay largely deserted. |
| Mazagán | [El Jadida (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Jadida) | Atlantic Moroccan port valued enough for Portugal to fortify it as Mazagão in 1514; the El Jadida name came with the nineteenth-century rebuilding. |
| Orán | [Oran (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oran) | Taken by Castile in 1509, it was the Kingdom of Tlemcen's main sea outlet for wool and grain. |
| Argel | [Algiers (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algiers) | Rising port of the central Maghreb; Spain seized its offshore Peñón in 1510, just before the corsair era transformed it. |
| Mostaganem | [Mostaganem (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mostaganem) | Harbour of the Tlemcen hinterland exporting grain and fish through the period. |
